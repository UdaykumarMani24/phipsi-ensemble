How the analyzer works

Phi/Psi Ensemble Analyzer · Developed by Dr. Udayakumar Mani & Dr. Senthilkumar Rathnasamy

1. The question it answers

Proteins are not rigid. Some regions hold one shape; others move. When several structures of the same protein exist (the 10–30 models of an NMR entry, or several crystal structures), comparing them shows which parts move. This tool measures that, residue by residue, and compares it with how confident AlphaFold is about each residue.

2. Backbone torsion angles φ and ψ

The shape of a protein backbone is set mainly by two rotatable bonds per residue:

  • φ (phi): rotation about the N–Cα bond, defined by atoms C(i−1), N, Cα, C.
  • ψ (psi): rotation about the Cα–C bond, defined by atoms N, Cα, C, N(i+1).

Each angle is a dihedral: the angle between the plane of the first three atoms and the plane of the last three. With bond vectors b1, b2, b3 and plane normals n1 = b1 × b2, n2 = b2 × b3:

angle = atan2( |b2| · (b1 · n2),  n1 · n2 )

This gives a signed angle from −180° to +180° (IUPAC convention). A helix has φ ≈ −60°, ψ ≈ −40°; a β-strand has φ ≈ −120°, ψ ≈ +130°. If the C–N distance between neighbouring residues is above 2 Å, the chain is broken there and that angle is left blank.

3. Step by step

  1. Load structures. PDB files are downloaded from the RCSB Protein Data Bank, or read from a file you upload. Every MODEL in the file is kept. Only backbone atoms (N, Cα, C) are used; for alternate locations, the first (“A”) is used.
  2. Compute angles. φ and ψ are calculated for every residue in every model.
  3. Measure the spread with circular statistics. Angles wrap around: −179° and +179° are only 2° apart. An ordinary average of those two would give 0°, which is wrong. So each angle is turned into a point on a circle (cos θ, sin θ) and the points are averaged:

    mean = atan2( Σ sin θi, Σ cos θi )     R = | average of (cos θi, sin θi) |     circular SD = √( −2 ln R )

    R is 1 when all models agree and falls towards 0 when the angles scatter.
  4. Flexibility score. For each residue, flexibility = average of the circular SD of φ and of ψ, in degrees. Near 0° means rigid; 60° or more is shown as fully flexible (red).
  5. Two-state check. Sometimes a residue switches between two distinct shapes (for example a peptide flip), instead of wobbling around one. The tool splits that residue’s (φ, ψ) points into two groups (2-means clustering on the circle). It marks the residue “2-state” when each group holds at least 15% of the models, the two group centres are at least 60° apart, and each group is tight compared with that distance. For such residues the single mean and SD are misleading, so both centres are drawn on the Ramachandran plot.
  6. AlphaFold comparison (optional). AlphaFold stores its per-residue confidence, pLDDT (0–100), in its model file. The tool reads it and pairs it with each residue (UniProt position = PDB residue number + offset).
  7. Correlation. Spearman’s rank correlation ρ is computed between flexibility and pLDDT. A negative ρ means flexible residues tend to have lower AlphaFold confidence. The p-value comes from 5000 random shuffles (permutation test). It is approximate, because neighbouring residues move together and are not independent.

4. Reading the results

  • Flexibility profile: one bar per residue, coloured blue (rigid) to red (flexible). The black line is AlphaFold pLDDT. An orange dot marks a two-state residue.
  • 3D view: the structure coloured the same way. All models are overlaid; tick “first model only” for a single one.
  • Ramachandran plot: grey points are every residue in every model. Tap a residue to see its points from each model in colour, with ✚ at the circular mean (or two orange ✚ for a two-state residue).
  • Scatter plot: flexibility against pLDDT, one dot per residue.
  • Table and CSV: all numbers per residue, and every angle of every model, for use in Excel, R or Python.

5. Good inputs to try

  • 1D3Z, chain A, UniProt P62987, offset 0: ubiquitin, 10 NMR models. Rigid core, flexible C-terminal tail.
  • 2K39, chain A, UniProt P62987, offset 0: ubiquitin, 116 models including slower motions. Look at Asp52.
  • 1DMO, chain A, UniProt P0DP33, offset 1: calcium-free calmodulin, 30 NMR models. Flexible central linker.
  • 1UBQ, 1UBI, chain A: two ubiquitin crystal structures compared with each other.

6. Limitations

  • The spread of an NMR ensemble reflects both real motion and how tightly the experimental data constrained the calculation. Treat it as “well defined vs poorly defined”, not as a direct measurement of motion.
  • Terminal residues have only one of the two angles, so their score uses that angle alone.
  • With multiple PDB entries, residues are matched by chain and residue number, so all entries must use the same numbering.
  • Only PDB-format files are read. Very large entries that exist only as mmCIF must be converted first.
  • pLDDT measures AlphaFold’s confidence, not motion directly. It tracks disorder well but can miss switching between two ordered states.

7. Privacy and validation

All calculations run on your own device; uploaded files are never sent anywhere. The angle calculation was checked against a synthetic ensemble built from known angles (largest error 0.13°, from rounding), and a script is included to compare results with Biopython.
